About

One operator, one method, one file that never closes.

BARSS is the working practice of Wesley Bertil. It started as a question about a single transaction, the 1825 indemnity, and turned into a documented corpus of more than twenty-five extraction cases with named perpetrators and modeled damages, because the same institutions kept surfacing in files that had nothing to do with each other.

The practice is built the way a forensic accounting shop is built and not the way an advocacy organization is built. Primary sources over secondary. Stated assumptions over asserted conclusions. Published corrections when a chain turns out to be wrong, including chains we built ourselves.

Haiti is the home case. The archive, the succession chains, the defendant tables, the live commission process, and the diaspora networks are mapped here in more depth than anywhere else. The rest of the corpus exists because a method that only works on one country is not a method.

Work has been built for and delivered to legal counsel, advocacy organizations, and academic reviewers. Engagements are taken directly.

How we work

Five commitments that shape every deliverable.

  • Primary sources, cited inline. If a figure cannot be traced to a document, it does not appear in the report.
  • Assumptions in the open. The model is written down so it can be attacked. A number you cannot audit is a number you cannot use.
  • Ranges, not single points. Low, central, and high, with the sensitivity that produced them.
  • Corrections published. Chains we ruled out stay visible, including our own errors. It costs credibility once and buys it permanently.
  • Findings, not accusations. Modeled exposure under a stated methodology. The legal conclusions belong to counsel.
The number has to survive the person who is paid to break it. Everything else is decoration.
